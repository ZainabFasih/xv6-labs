Q: For every PTE in the print_pgtbl output, explain what it logically
contains and what its permission bits are.

Each line shows: va (virtual address), pte (raw entry), pa (physical
address from the PTE's PPN), and perm (the low flag bits).

PTE flags (riscv.h): PTE_V=0x01 valid, PTE_R=0x02 read, PTE_W=0x04 write,
PTE_X=0x08 execute, PTE_U=0x10 user, PTE_A=0x40 accessed, PTE_D=0x80 dirty.

- Low virtual addresses (va 0, 0x1000, ...) are the user program's text,
  data and stack. perm 0x1B = V|R|W|U; perm 0x5B = that plus the ACCESSED
  bit. Text is read+execute+user; data/stack is read+write+user.

- Lines with pte 0x0 / pa 0x0 / perm 0x0 are UNMAPPED pages (no valid PTE),
  i.e. gaps in the address space such as guard-page regions.

- High virtual addresses are the special top-of-space pages:
    * perm 0xC7 = V|R|W|A|D, PTE_U NOT set  -> TRAPFRAME: kernel-only R/W.
    * perm 0x4B = V|R|X|A,  PTE_U NOT set  -> TRAMPOLINE: kernel-only R/X;
      the same physical page is mapped into every process at the top.

Each PTE maps one 4096-byte virtual page to one physical page and records
what the page may be used for. xv6 does not place virtual pages
consecutively in physical memory, so consecutive va map to scattered pa.
